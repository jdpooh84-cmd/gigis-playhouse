# THE FIRST CIRCLE — Pitch Script (to read aloud)

*A warm, spoken-word pitch of the movie idea. Roughly 6–8 minutes read at a natural pace.*

---

**Opening**

I want to share a kids' movie idea with you. It's an animated film — think colorful, full of music and movement — made for children around 7 to 12 and for families to watch together on a movie night. I've been working on it for a while now, and I really want to tell you the whole thing, start to finish, and hear what you think.

So pour something to drink, and let me walk you through it.

---

**Title and Logline**

The working title is *The First Circle*.

Here's the heart of it in one breath: a twelve-year-old boy named Daniel, who feels invisible everywhere he goes, talks his way into the biggest dance competition in the world to finally be seen — but he discovers the contest is hiding something ancient and powerful, and the only way to truly win is to do the one thing the competition was secretly built to stop: bring everyone together instead of beating them.

It's for kids 7 to 12, but it's really for the whole family. The kind of movie where the kids are dancing along and the parents are quietly wiping their eyes by the end.

---

**Characters and World**

Let me introduce you to Daniel first. He's twelve, full of energy, funny, kind — the kid everybody *likes* but nobody truly *sees*. His mind moves fast and to a rhythm all its own, which gets him in trouble in normal rooms. He's got family from two worlds — his mom's side from Spain, his dad's side from Nigeria — and a house full of love. But more than anything, Daniel just wants to matter to someone. He wants to be picked. He wants to be *seen*.

Then there's Stella. She's twelve too, and she sees the world in a way no one else does — patterns, shapes, the way people move, the moment a rhythm is about to break. She notices everything. She's not there to help Daniel win; she's her own person, with her own question about where she belongs. She becomes the one who figures out what this competition really is.

There's Nia, a girl on Daniel's crew with the most beautiful voice you've ever heard — except she stopped singing after she lost someone she loved. Her voice carries the whole emotional heart of the movie. And there's Theo, the steady one who holds the group together, and Lucky, who's part Mexican and part Japanese and feels like she belongs to neither — until she learns she's a bridge between both.

Their main rival is Goliath — the reigning world champion. He's not a cartoon bad guy. He's a kid raised to believe that the way you get respect is by making everybody else smaller. He genuinely thinks that's just how the world works. He doesn't know his own family is being used by something darker than ambition. He's not a villain. He's a warning of who Daniel could become.

And the world? Picture something bright and playful and gorgeous — Pixar-warm — but with this competition that feels as huge as the Olympics. Five regions from all around the globe, each one dancing its own story, its own culture, its own answer to "where do I belong." And underneath all the lights and the stage, there's an older, quieter magic humming in the background.

---

**Story: Beginning, Middle, and End**

Here's how it goes.

In the beginning, we meet Daniel in his everyday life — too much energy for every classroom, loved at home but unseen out in the world. Then everything changes when he stumbles into a doorway to this enormous global dance competition. On a wild, impulsive leap, he enters — with no team and no plan — just a desperate hope that *this* is the place he'll finally be noticed.

In the middle, he builds a crew out of the kids nobody else wanted — Stella, Nia, Theo, Lucky. And they're good. Really good. But as they climb, two things start happening at once. They start winning — and winning starts asking Daniel to make himself smaller, quieter, more "acceptable," until he's about to lose the very thing that made him *him*. And Stella starts to notice that something is wrong underneath the competition. The music goes strange in certain rooms. Dancers fall out of sync without knowing why. There's a shadow in the building that feeds on people being divided and alone.

They learn the truth: this competition used to be something beautiful and ancient — the First Circle — where people of every culture once danced together, not to compete, but to protect each other. And something out there has spent centuries twisting it into a contest of one winner and everybody else left feeling like a loser. Because as long as everyone's fighting to be on top, that shadow stays strong.

There's a hard middle stretch where Daniel chooses the trophy over his friends, and it costs him everything. He makes himself small, and he *still* feels invisible. That's his lowest point.

And that's where the ending begins. Just when the audience thinks the big finale is arriving — everything goes quiet. Daniel is alone, stripped of everything he thought he'd earned, starting over from nothing. And he starts to sing. Just him, raw and honest. Then Nia — who hasn't sung in so long — steps in and answers him. Her voice lifts the whole thing up. It's the sound of hope coming back after the worst moment.

For the big finish, Daniel and his crew don't win the competition the way it was designed to be won. Instead, they do the thing the shadow was built to prevent: they get all five crews, from every corner of the world, to dance *together* — no rankings, no winners, no losers — just everyone, in rhythm, at the same time. And in that moment the shadow breaks. Goliath sees, for the first time, what real belonging looks like. And Daniel finally gets the thing he's wanted all along. He's seen. Not for winning — just for being exactly who he is.

---

**Themes and Message**

At its core, this movie is about belonging — about going from feeling invisible to finally being seen. It's about the idea that you don't have to make yourself smaller to be loved, and that real strength isn't beating other people, it's bringing them with you.

There's courage in it, and friendship, and a quiet thread of faith running underneath — never preachy, never spelled out, but there for anyone who feels it. The kind of thing where grown-ups recognize something deeper and kids just feel it as warmth and wonder.

I want kids to walk out feeling braver about being themselves. And I want parents to walk out remembering what it felt like to want to belong — and feeling grateful their kids are hearing this message.

---

**What Makes It Special**

Here's the thing I think makes this stand out. Most kids' movies about a competition end with the hero winning the trophy. Ours ends with the hero realizing the trophy was never the point — that the real victory is everyone dancing together. That flip is the whole soul of the movie.

And then there's the music. The whole film is built on a rich, soulful, R&B-rooted sound — and there's one anthem at the center that I genuinely believe families would sing for years. It even has a clap-and-stomp version simple enough to do in a living room, a classroom, or a school gym — no instruments needed. It's the kind of song that turns a movie into something kids carry around with them. That's the dream: a story *and* a song that outlive the screen.

---

**Closing**

That's the heart of the movie. It's called *The First Circle*, and it's about a boy who goes from invisible to seen, and a world that remembers how to dance together.

I'd really love to know what you think — honestly — and whether you can see us making this together.
