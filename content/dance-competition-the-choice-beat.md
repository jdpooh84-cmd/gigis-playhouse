# THE FIRST CIRCLE — "THE CHOICE" BEAT (Act 3 replacement for the faked injury)

*Resolves Supervisor Flag 3 (Part 5 §E). Replaces the overplayed "faked injury / noble hand-off" with a beat built on recognition, not generosity. Developed through the Narrative (02), Character (03), Scene (07), Theme (01), and Moral-Architecture lenses, and verified against the moral physics. Status: PROPOSED → pending Showrunner (Justin & Rachel) lock.*

---

## WHY THE OLD VERSION FAILED (named, so we don't drift back)

- **The "this is yours" hand-off** makes the moment about the *giver's* nobility and renders Daniel a passive recipient — he didn't earn the stage, someone gifted it. It also quietly diminishes him: you're only here because the better dancer let you be.
- **The faked injury** rewards a deception inside a film whose physics honor truth (Moral Architecture: "truth arrives… it changes everything"; "wrong actually costs something"). A trick at the hinge of the climax poisons the well.

Both are replaced.

---

## THE BETTER BEAT — RECOGNITION, NOT GENEROSITY

**One line:** the crew's lead goes down for real, the decision of who replaces her falls to the one person trained to see what the scoreboard can't — and against every obvious choice in the room, she chooses Daniel, with total certainty, because she already saw the truth of him once and never forgot it.

### The mechanics (no deception anywhere)
1. **A real injury — and an attack.** The crew's veteran lead, **Reyna** (the technically flawless one — the rubric's idea of "great"), goes down for real, mid-final. It is **the Fracture's move**: collapse the strongest crew before the Circle can form. So the injury is honest *and* raises the supernatural stakes — no trick, real danger, the enemy acting. (Moral physics intact: no lie is rewarded; the Fracture's division-move is what's in motion.)
2. **The room has obvious answers — and Daniel isn't one of them.** Senior, polished dancers are right there; the safe pick is obvious. Daniel has spent the whole film *hiding in the back*, not the lead, easy to overlook — exactly as he's been overlooked his entire life.
3. **The choice falls to Coach Wren** (the mentor who *quit the rubric* because it could never measure the real thing — established Part 2). Every logical signal says take the polished veteran.
4. **She chooses Daniel — and she is not guessing.** This is the whole beat. She doesn't hope; she *recognizes.* The crew protests ("He's not even—"). She doesn't argue the case. She already knows.

### The certainty is *earned* (the plant)
Earlier — a quiet night in the Undercommons, or the end of Level Two — **Wren secretly saw Daniel dance his true self** (to Mamá Àbèní's hum, alone, when he thought no one was watching). She said nothing then. So at the crisis she has no hesitation: she saw the real thing once, and you don't forget the real thing. *(Moral physics: truth arrives as a moment — Wren's certainty was bought by a truth she witnessed earlier, not by a speech.)*

### The line (certainty, never "take a chance")
Kill any version of "I'm taking a chance on you" or "this is yours." The register is **recognition**:
- Crew, panicking: *"Coach — who do we put in? Mari, or—"*
- Wren, already looking at the back of the room, at Daniel: *"It's him."*
- *"…Daniel? He's not even—"*
- Wren, quiet, final: **"I'm not guessing. I saw it. It was always going to be him."**

No pep talk. No gamble. A fact she's known since the night she watched him.

### The image
A room full of people scanning for the safe answer — and one person turning to look at **the kid no one was looking at.** Hold on Daniel's face: not triumph. Disbelief. The boy who was never picked, hearing his name first.

---

## WHY THIS LANDS HARDER THAN THE HAND-OFF

- **It pays off Daniel's exact wound.** His whole life: *never chosen, never seen.* Here he is chosen *first*, at the highest stakes, by the one person with the eye to see him. The theme isn't stated — it *happens.*
- **He still has to earn it.** Wren's choice is recognition, not a free pass. He now has to *prove the seeing true* — which is what the praise dance becomes: not a bid to win, but a man honoring the people who saw him (Wren, Mamá Àbèní, and TÌ on the floor behind him).
- **The Fracture's attack backfires — the moral physics close.** It injured the lead to *divide and collapse* the crew; instead it forced forward the one dancer who can *complete the First Circle.* Domination loses the thing it tried to protect (Moral law). The enemy's move becomes its undoing.
- **Nobody is diminished.** Reyna isn't a noble martyr and Daniel isn't a charity case. A great dancer fell; a true one was recognized. Both keep their dignity.
